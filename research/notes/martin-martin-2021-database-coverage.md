# Google Scholar, Microsoft Academic, Scopus, Dimensions, Web of Science, and OpenCitations' COCI: a multidisciplinary comparison of coverage via citations (Martín-Martín, Thelwall, Orduna-Malea & Delgado López-Cózar, 2021)

- **Source:** Scientometrics 126(1): 871–906 · https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-020-03690-4 (accepted manuscript, arXiv:2004.14329; pages = PDF pages)
- **File:** `../papers/martin-martin-2021-coverage-six-sources.pdf`
- **Licence:** arXiv non-exclusive licence: local only.
- **Evidence:** empirical · 3,073,351 citations to 2,515 highly cited 2006 papers in 252 subject categories (p. 1).

## What it says
- Google Scholar found 88% of all citations, and 89–94% of those found by each other source; Microsoft Academic
  (the data OpenAlex was built from) found 60%; Dimensions 54%; COCI 28% (p. 1).
- Gaps in the other sources are largest in some Humanities categories and Physics (p. 1).
- Google Scholar indexes automatically "any seemingly academic document" and offers no bulk export (p. 2–3).

## What it means for the plugin
- Supports adding Google Scholar results through the user's own export: it finds what the open databases miss,
  especially in the humanities and for older works.
- Supports reporting citation counts per database instead of one number: counts differ systematically by source.

## Quotes
- "Google Scholar is still the most comprehensive source." (p. 1)
