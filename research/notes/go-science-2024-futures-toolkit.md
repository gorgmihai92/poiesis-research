## What it says
- Horizon scanning is the systematic collection of insights on emerging trends and weak signals of change
  ("Horizon-Scanning").
- The risk it names: missing important changes by not scanning widely enough ("What to watch out for").
- Scans are stored in a common format, with metadata such as date, author and source link ("Step 3").
- Each scan says what it is about, how it relates to the policy area, and why the scanner thinks it matters
  ("Step 3").
- Tips: look at the original source, link to it, count the scans in each category so that one kind, such as
  technology, does not crowd out the rest, and include an item even when it seems irrelevant at first ("Tips").
- A scan can be a structured record in place of a one-page article ("Alternative Approaches").
- AI systems can help by gathering the basic information, which is then structured ("Tips").

## What it means for the plugin
- A signal note in this plugin is the record: what was observed, where and when, with the link to the original.
  How it relates to the project and why it matters are the scanner's reading, which this plugin leaves to
  poiesis-foresight.
- `signal-scout` reports how many signals came from each kind of source, so a scan that leans on one kind shows.
- Practitioner guidance: it gives a format to borrow, not evidence that scanning works.
